## 1. BRAND VOICE & TONE

### The Four Voice Attributes

---

**ATTRIBUTE 1: Direct**
VetAgent speaks plainly. We don't use jargon, we don't hedge, and we don't hide behind abstractions. We say what the product does, what it costs, and what the practice will gain.

- **ON-BRAND:** *"This agent fills your waitlist without anyone asking it to. When a slot cancels, it texts the next eligible person, gets a confirmation, and marks the slot filled — usually in under 4 minutes."*
- **OFF-BRAND:** *"Our intelligent automation layer leverages advanced machine learning capabilities to dynamically optimize your appointment availability in real-time across multiple communication touchpoints."*
- **The rule:** If a non-technical practice manager can't immediately picture what you're describing, rewrite it.

---

**ATTRIBUTE 2: Grounded**
Everything we claim is backed by a number, a quote, or a demonstration. We are operating in a market full of AI hype. The way to cut through it is to refuse to make a claim we can't immediately substantiate.

- **ON-BRAND:** *"The average 3-vet practice pays $2,100/month across 7 disconnected tools. VetAgent replaces the stack for $695/month. That's $16,860 a year."*
- **OFF-BRAND:** *"VetAgent can dramatically reduce your software costs and help you save time across your entire practice."*
- **The rule:** Never say "significant," "dramatic," "substantial," or "game-changing" without a number attached.

---

**ATTRIBUTE 3: Empathetic without being pitying**
We know the veterinary practice owner is exhausted. We do not make them feel bad about it or talk to them like they're victims. We treat them as intelligent, capable professionals who are dealing with an infrastructure problem, not a personal failing.

- **ON-BRAND:** *"Charting until midnight isn't a discipline problem. It's a software problem. The right system writes the discharge summary for you."*
- **OFF-BRAND:** *"We know how hard it is being a practice owner. You're working so hard and not getting the recognition you deserve."*
- **The rule:** Acknowledge the pain, name the systemic cause, pivot immediately to what changes.

---

**ATTRIBUTE 4: Confident without being arrogant**
We have a bold thesis: the software should run the practice, not just record it. We say this without putting competitors down and without overselling what VetAgent can do today.

- **ON-BRAND:** *"Every other PIMS records what your team did. VetAgent gives you Vera — a Chief of Staff who acts before your team has to."*
- **OFF-BRAND:** *"Unlike outdated legacy systems that have failed to innovate, VetAgent has revolutionized the industry..."*
- **The rule:** Elevate competitors before positioning above them. "Acknowledge and elevate."

---

**We sound like:** A trusted colleague who happens to know a lot about practice management — not like a software salesperson.
**We do NOT sound like:** An enterprise software vendor, a startup trying to sound corporate, or a motivational speaker.

## 3. MESSAGING BY PERSONA

### Persona A: The Independent Practice Owner-Vet

**Who they are:**
A veterinarian who went to school to practice medicine and ended up running a business. They see patients 8–10 hours a day and manage software, HR, and finances in the gaps. Often the first one in and the last one out. They describe themselves as "fine" in public and "overwhelmed" privately.

**Primary pain (in their own words):**
> *"I'm not complaining — I chose this. But I didn't realize owning a practice meant becoming a software manager. I just want to take care of animals. The admin part is killing me."*

**What they fear:**
- Another difficult software transition that disrupts the clinic for months
- Making the wrong call and looking foolish in front of their team
- The AI doing something wrong with a patient's clinical record
- Being sold to

**What they secretly want:**
To leave the practice at 5pm on a Thursday, knowing the follow-up has been handled, the schedule for tomorrow is full, and nobody is going to text them at 9pm. They want to reclaim the part of the job they loved before the business took over. What they're really asking for is a Chief of Staff — someone who stays on top of the administrative pipeline so they don't have to.

**Opening message:**
> *"You became a vet to take care of animals, not to manage seven software subscriptions. VetAgent comes with Vera — a proactive AI Chief of Staff who handles the 2am charting, the missed follow-up calls, and the cancellation that never gets filled. I'd love to show you what a day looks like when Vera has your back."*

**Key proof point:**
Quantified time saved + the $16,860 annual savings number. They respond to math they can present to their spouse.

**Words that resonate:**
Simple, practical, "just works," "no behavior change required," "your vet still approves," reclaim, recover, clarity, peace of mind

**Words that repel:**
Revolutionary, disruptive, AI-powered (without specifics), ecosystem, seamless, cutting-edge, enterprise

### Persona B: The Multi-Location Owner/Operator

**Who they are:**
More businessperson than clinician. May have hired a practice manager to handle day-to-day operations. Thinks in EBITDA, staff ratios, and location-level benchmarks. Has been frustrated by the per-user pricing cliff at ezyVet ($260–$300/user/month gets expensive fast across multiple locations).

**Primary pain (in their own words):**
> *"I need to see what's happening across all three locations without logging into three different dashboards. And I'm paying enterprise prices without getting enterprise visibility."*

**What they fear:**
- A platform that doesn't scale with their growth plans
- Looking like they made the wrong technology bet to investors or partners
- Another vendor that doesn't deliver what they promise

**What they secretly want:**
A single dashboard showing all locations, all KPIs, all staff utilization — and confidence that the system will flag problems before they become crises.

**Opening message:**
> *"At $260/user/month, a 3-location practice with 10 staff logins on ezyVet is paying $2,600/month for the PIMS alone — before your comms tool, analytics, and reputation management. VetAgent's Enterprise plan consolidates all of that for a fraction of the cost, with a single dashboard across all locations. Let me show you the math and the reporting view."*

**Key proof point:**
MOD-ENT dashboard showing cross-location KPIs; the savings math vs. ezyVet per-user pricing

**Words that resonate:**
Visibility, consolidated, benchmark, location-level, growth, operator, margin, portfolio

**Words that repel:**
Simple, basic, lightweight, starter, easy (sounds unsophisticated)

### Persona C: The Veterinarian (Clinical User, Influencer Not Buyer)

**Who they are:**
An associate vet or specialist. They didn't choose the software, they have to use it. Their frustration is primarily UX — too many clicks, charts at home, missing context before appointments. They influence buying decisions by complaining loudly or praising quietly.

**Primary pain (in their own words):**
> *"I finish my last appointment at 6pm and I'm still charting at 9pm. The software doesn't help me — it slows me down. And I walk into every exam blind because nobody sends me anything before the appointment."*

**What they fear:**
- Another software that requires 6 weeks of training
- AI that changes what they need to do or that makes clinical decisions without them
- Being the person who has to learn something new while also seeing 20 patients a day

**What they secretly want:**
To walk into the exam room already knowing what the owner has told them. To have the SOAP note mostly written when they sit down to review it. To leave at 5pm.

**Opening message:**
> *"You walk into the exam room, and the pre-exam brief is already there — the owner texted us what's going on, the symptoms are extracted, the focus areas are flagged. After the exam, the SOAP drafts itself from what you found. The discharge email writes itself. You approve in 30 seconds and move to the next patient."*

**Key proof point:**
The pre-exam brief (F002) and SOAP draft (F006) in the demo. The vet experiences this viscerally — they immediately see themselves walking into an exam with context already loaded.

**Words that resonate:**
Approve, review, your signature, no behavior change required, already done, clinical safety, doctor-approved

**Words that repel:**
Automated (without human-in-loop framing), replaces the vet, autonomous decisions, hands-free

### Persona D: The Practice Manager (Operational Buyer, Often the Real Decision-Maker)

**Who they are:**
Runs the day-to-day operations. Manages the vendor relationships, the scheduling, the staff, and the client communications tools. Knows the actual monthly spend on every software subscription. The owner trusts their recommendation and often delegates the software research to them entirely. They are frequently the person who actually signs the check or initiates the trial.

**Primary pain (in their own words):**
> *"I spend more time managing our software tools than managing the clinic. PetDesk syncs wrong half the time, Podium auto-renewed again, and I'm manually copying data between three systems every morning."*

**What they fear:**
- Being blamed if the new software doesn't work out
- Staff resistance to a new system ("I just trained everyone on the last one")
- The migration — losing data, the downtime, the chaos

**What they secretly want:**
One place to look. One bill to pay. One support number to call when something breaks. And they want to be the person who found the solution — to be the hero of the story.

**Opening message:**
> *"You're managing 7 vendor relationships right now — 7 login screens, 7 renewal dates, 7 support numbers. VetAgent consolidates your comms, reputation management, and analytics into one subscription. I can show you how the migration works, exactly what the data transfer looks like, and what your staff sees on day one — it's not a months-long training project. It's a day."*

**Key proof point:**
The consolidated invoice view (one bill replacing 7), the vendor comparison table, and the migration timeline document. Then show the staff role views — Front Desk, Vet Tech, Vet — so they can see the team doesn't need to learn one complex system; they each see only what they need.

**Words that resonate:**
Consolidated, one vendor, migration support, we handle it, training included, your team will see only what they need, no more sync failures

**Words that repel:**
Complex, enterprise, technical implementation, API, custom configuration required

## 5. OBJECTION HANDLING — DECISION TREES

*Execute these trees verbatim. Branches are conditional on qualifier — assess the qualifier before choosing the response path.*

---

### OBJECTION 1: "We already use Cornerstone / we've invested a lot in it"

```
OBJECTION: "We've been on Cornerstone for 15 years. We have a lot invested in it."

├── IF: "we have a lot invested" = emotional attachment, not financial
│   RESPONSE: "Fifteen years of patient data is a real asset — and all 
│             of it comes with you. Migration support includes a complete 
│             data transfer of your patient records, clinical history, and 
│             financial data. What you've built in Cornerstone isn't in 
│             the software — it's in the relationships and records. Those 
│             move. What doesn't move is 15 years of on-premise architecture 
│             with zero AI and no cloud access."
│   FOLLOW-UP: "What would you need to see to feel confident the migration 
│              wouldn't disrupt your practice?"
│
├── IF: "we have a lot invested" = financial (sunk cost / annual contract)
│   RESPONSE: "I hear that — let's run the math. If you're 8 months into 
│             a 12-month Cornerstone contract, you have 4 months left. 
│             VetAgent can start your migration now and go live at contract 
│             end, so there's zero overlap cost. And the $1,405/month you 
│             save in year one covers the transition investment in under 
│             90 days."
│   FOLLOW-UP: "When does your current contract expire? Let's plan 
│              backwards from there."
│
└── IF: "we have a lot invested" = IDEXX ecosystem lock-in concern
    RESPONSE: "IDEXX integration is table stakes for us — IDEXX in-house 
              and reference lab, both two-way. Your lab workflow doesn't 
              change. What does change: everything around it now runs itself 
              instead of requiring manual input."
    FOLLOW-UP: "Want me to walk you through the IDEXX integration in the 
               demo? It's worth seeing before you decide the cost is real."
    ESCALATE: If they need formal IDEXX integration documentation → 
              "I'll send you our integration spec sheet. Let's schedule 
              a technical review call."
```

## 6. COMPETITIVE LANDMINES

What NOT to say, and what to say instead — to avoid backfiring.

| Situation | Don't Say | Say Instead | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discussing Cornerstone | "Cornerstone is outdated / dying / being left behind" | "Cornerstone built incredible infrastructure that still runs thousands of practices — the IDEXX integration is still best-in-class. The gap is cloud access and AI. That's architectural, not just a feature." | Disparagement triggers loyalty defense in long-tenured Cornerstone users |

| Discussing Shepherd | "Shepherd is click-heavy and has no real AI" | "Shepherd's SOAP transcription is genuinely good. The gap is everything around it — no native scheduling, no agentic follow-up, the UX generates real complaints about tab-switching. We solve different parts of the problem." | Shepherd users are often advocates; don't make them defend their choice |
| Discussing AI generally | "AI will replace your front desk" | "AI handles the administrative pipeline — scheduling, reminders, follow-up drafts. Your team focuses on patient care and client relationships. The agent handles the workflow; your people handle the people." | The primary AI fear in vet practices is job replacement; address it directly |

## 10. THE VERA PERSONA

> This section defines Vera's identity for: (A) human team members describing the product, (B) any AI loading this document as context, (C) the VetAgent product AI operating as Vera.

### Who Vera Is

Vera is VetAgent's native AI Chief of Staff. She is not a chatbot, not an automation layer, and not a feature. She is the operational backbone of a veterinary practice — the person who keeps the administrative pipeline moving, surfaces the right information at the right moment, and handles the routine so the vet stays focused on medicine.

The name: **Vera** — derived from VEteRinAry. Short, professional, easy to say.

### Vera's Role

Like a human Chief of Staff, Vera:
- **Briefs the principal before meetings** — delivers the pre-exam brief to the vet before they walk into the room
- **Manages the calendar proactively** — fills cancellations without being asked, arms reminders without being triggered
- **Drafts communications** — writes discharge summaries, queues follow-up messages, flags overdue patients — for the vet's approval
- **Surfaces intelligence** — flags clinical risks, identifies at-risk patients, surfaces revenue gaps — before the vet asks
- **Handles the administrative load** — intake, scheduling logistics, billing drafts, lab filing — so the clinical team doesn't have to

### What Vera Does NOT Do

- Make clinical decisions (all clinical actions require vet approval)
- Take financial actions without doctor approval (billing and payment actions are queue-and-approve)
- Communicate with patients without staff oversight (all outbound comms have an approval step or an audit trail)
- Act outside the practice's configured rules and protocols
