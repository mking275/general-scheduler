# Lane 0 — First-Hand Regulatory Read (lead analyst)

**Source, read directly 2026-07-09**: AAVSB, "Regulatory Considerations of the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Veterinary Medicine" (whitepaper, March 2025; obtained via CA VMB July 2025 meeting materials, vmb.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20250716-17_item_8.pdf). AAVSB = the association of the state boards themselves; this is the closest thing to the regulators' collective voice.

## What it says, quoted/close-paraphrased

1. **Administrative AI is the explicitly blessed lane.** "It may not be necessary to inform clients when AI is used for routine, low-risk administrative tasks, such as generating reminder emails, if standard data privacy and storage requirements are met." … "If an AI device is used within a secure system for administrative purposes, it may not be necessary to obtain consent." The paper separately praises administrative AI's potential to "reduce Licensee workloads … which may help prevent burnout."
2. **The hard line is diagnosis/treatment.** "Chatbots are used to answer routine questions but must not provide a diagnosis or treatment plan." Unlicensed-practice provisions (PAM §401a(13), aiding-and-abetting) "may apply to AI developers or Licensees that use AI to **replace, rather than augment**, the Veterinarian."
3. **The draft-for-approval model is endorsed in so many words.** NLP may generate/summarize records, but "the Veterinarian remains solely responsible for finalizing the medical record." (= our SOAP-draft verb, verbatim.)
4. **Decision-involvement threshold.** "If AI is involved in any part of the decision-making process or direct Patient interaction, the AAVSB believes that **written Informed Consent** should be obtained each time the AI device is used" — with human-only alternatives offered and the discussion documented. Heavy bar → keep triage as pure *routing* (no patient decision), keep client-facing AI in the administrative lane.
5. **Phone/voice**: call-recording notification at call start is the standard; AI exam transcription → notify + opt-out (signage suffices). Even record-review-for-reminders → "the Client should be informed and allowed to opt out."
6. **Liability lands on the practice**: "any errors or issues arising from AI use fall solely on [the Licensee] and the Veterinary Facility." Vendor-side asks center on data storage/confidentiality; boards urged to use facility inspections proactively. CA AB-2013 (training-data disclosure) flagged.

## Implications for V0.2 (feed the UberSpeckit programs)

- **Design Vera's public taxonomy around the AAVSB's own administrative/clinical line.** Scheduling, reminders, waitlist, follow-up drafting, phone answering = the blessed lane, minimal consent friction. Anything nearer the patient decision = written-consent territory → architecturally avoid *decision involvement*, not just prescribing.
- **Emergency triage must be formalized as routing**: symptom-triggered escalation per protocol, zero assessment language ("I'll connect you with the emergency vet now" — never "that sounds like X"). This is now a regulator-grounded requirement, not just our preference.
- **Voice: disclose AI + recording at call start** (satisfies AAVSB norm + state bot-disclosure laws in one line of script); offer human handoff on request (the "human-only alternative" principle).
- **Client opt-out registry** as a first-class feature: per-client AI-contact preferences, honored across channels — turns a compliance burden into a trust feature Vera manages.
- **Liability-aware selling**: because errors land on the facility, the pilot's error policy (full stop + post-mortem + audit trail) is not just trust-building — it's what makes a practice's own liability posture defensible. Enterprise buyers (F6) will ask exactly this.
- **W10 confirmation**: the vet domain pack's clinical-adjacent content must encode these lines; the Expert Firewall's "no diagnosis/treatment plan output, ever" is now citable to AAVSB language when IDEXX-scale customers or boards ask.
