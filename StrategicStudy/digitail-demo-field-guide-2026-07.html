Digitail Demo — Field Question Guide

Every question below is normal buyer diligence — but each one is aimed at a specific claim from our competitive research. The "listen for" lines tell you what the answer reveals. Let them drive; let the silences talk.
Prepared by VetAgent · July 2026 · For the Digitail product demonstration
If time is short — the five that matter

#3 financial migration guarantee · #5 largest live group · #6 multi-location setup, live on screen · #8 what runs with nobody clicking · #9 who answers the phone

01 · Money

Get the real number on the record

Q1
What's the all-in monthly number for our 23 clinics — PIMS, AI, enterprise reporting, VoIP, everything? No surprises later?
Listen for: whether "AI included" survives a real quote. Their public marketing says all-inclusive; their plan tiers list the AI as a paid add-on with quote-only pricing. Make them reconcile the two out loud.
Q2
What's the contract term — and what happens to our pricing and our data at renewal?
Listen for: annual lock-in, renewal escalators, and any hesitation on the data question (it previews Q11).
02 · Migration

Their best pitch — and their worst documented failure

Q3 ★
Does our complete financial history migrate — accounts receivable, open client balances, payment history, inventory history? Will our balances reconcile to the penny on day one, and will you guarantee that in writing?
Listen for: this is the kill question. Their own migration marketing promises clinical and scheduling data and is conspicuously silent on financials — and the worst customer story on record is a group whose open balances didn't transfer, at a cost of thousands. A confident written yes would be genuinely new information. Watch the body language.
Q4
How many groups have you migrated off ezyVet specifically — not Cornerstone, not Avimark — ezyVet? Can we reference one?
Listen for: their wins come overwhelmingly from legacy on-premise systems. Migrations from a modern cloud platform like yours are a different animal. Named references or hedging — either answer is useful.
03 · Scale

Their largest known deployment is 22 hospitals. You are 23.

Q5 ★
Who's your largest group live today — and can I call them?
Listen for: if the answer is Veterinary United (~22 hospitals, Michigan), their ceiling hasn't moved in a year — and Synergy Vet would be the largest deployment they have ever attempted.
Q6 ★
Show us live, right now in the demo: set up an employee who works at three of our locations, then show me what a regional manager sees rolled up across all 23.
Listen for: per-location employee setup is the single most-cited complaint from multi-location customers at exactly this scale. Insist on live software, not slides. If they defer it to a follow-up, that's the answer.
Q7
What does support look like for a group our size — named account manager, response-time commitments, escalation path?
Listen for: reviews show excellent support for small practices and the opposite for 11–50-employee organizations. Ask for the SLA in the contract, not the intention.
04 · The AI, honestly

Separate what it does from what it drafts

Q8 ★
Walk me through what the AI completes entirely on its own, end to end, with nobody reviewing or clicking approve. Not drafts — finished actions.
Listen for: their own documentation says client messages "will not be sent unless you click the send button." After the "AI agents" pitch, get the draft-for-review reality stated plainly. The honest answer is: reminders and summaries run alone; everything meaningful waits for a human.
Q9 ★
When a client calls at two in the morning — or when the front desk is slammed at two in the afternoon — does your system answer the phone?
Listen for: it doesn't. Their phone story is a human-answered softphone with AI summaries afterward; actual AI answering is handled by third-party partners they refer to. Whether they say that plainly or imply otherwise is a character read on the whole company.
Q10
Whose AI models are under the hood, what accuracy numbers do you publish, and who is liable when the AI writes something wrong into a medical record?
Listen for: the models are rented (OpenAI, Anthropic, others — their own engineering FAQ says so); no published accuracy evaluations exist. The liability answer matters for your risk posture regardless of vendor.
05 · Exit & openness

The questions a software owner asks before signing anything

Q11
Is there a public API? QuickBooks or Xero integration? And if this doesn't work out in two years — how do we get everything back out, in what format, at what cost?
Listen for: no public developer API, no Zapier, and no accounting integration surfaced in our research. The exit answer tells you whether "your data" means yours.
Q12
Where are you on SOC 2?
Listen for: they don't have it. A timeline, a deflection, or a blank look — each is worth noting for a group your size.
06 · If the room is warm

Two bonus probes

Q13
Could our doctors use just the AI scribe, without migrating the whole practice system?
Listen for: this invites their standalone scribe pitch (Tails VIP) — their own admission that rip-and-replace is a hard sell. Their pricing and enthusiasm here reveal how they think about exactly the adoption path they can't own.
Q14
What's shipping in the next six months?
Listen for: anything about phone answering, "new clinical agents," enterprise features, or compliance certifications. Whatever they name goes on our watch list with a date next to it.
Tradecraft
Everything above is ordinary buyer diligence. Don't mention specific partner vendors, their investors, or engineering details by name — those only come out of someone who's read a competitive teardown. Ask open questions; let them volunteer. And record the screen share if possible — the live multi-location attempt (Q6) is the moment worth capturing most.
